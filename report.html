About the report

Economic impacts of solid waste management in India

This project examines what mismanaged waste can cost and how segregation-based recovery may create economic value.

1. The problem

Municipal solid waste creates collection, transport, processing and disposal challenges. When waste is mixed or poorly managed, useful materials may be lost and environmental burdens can increase.

2. Economic impacts

Waste management has costs across collection, transport, labour, sorting and disposal. Recovery can create value when materials are sufficiently clean, buyers are available and operating costs are controlled.

3. Environmental and health concerns

Poorly managed waste can contribute to pollution and environmental exposure. The health statistics displayed on the dashboard are reported comparisons; they should be interpreted in context and do not, by themselves, establish causation.

4. Source segregation

Separating recyclable, organic and residual waste can make it easier to route each stream to suitable recovery or disposal systems. Outcomes depend on participation, collection reliability and material quality.

5. The proposed solution

Waste2Value explores a small segregation-based recovery operation: receive separated material, sort and record it, and send suitable recovered material to buyers or processors.

6. Economic feasibility

The business case should compare material sales and service income against labour, transport, rent, equipment, maintenance, compliance and other operating costs. The companion surplus calculator lets you test an illustrative version of these numbers.

7. Baseline validation, limitations and next steps

The dashboard figures come from different publications and may use different definitions, locations and reporting periods. The pilot finances are baseline planning assumptions, not verified market quotes or actual results. For example, the 38% dry recyclable estimate assumes well-segregated collection, and the real share may be closer to the 20% to 25% commonly cited for municipal waste.

The first three months of a pilot would focus on field calibration. Household surveys, written rate cards and buyer agreements, route expense tracking and a waste audit would replace the planning assumptions (household fee, recyclable and compost prices, running costs, waste mix and wages) with measured local data. A real pilot would also need relevant regulatory checks.
